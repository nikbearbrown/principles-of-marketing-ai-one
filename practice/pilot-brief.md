# Independent marketing plan: a pilot someone can check

Build a one-page plan for a fictional library that lends portable bicycle repair kits. Use the [case data](../data/pilot-windows.json). The site has a staffed window and can fill at most eight requests per window. No actual contact, spend or live outreach is part of this exercise.

1. **Target.** Name a reachable group with a specific task, one reasonable alternative, and one finding that would make you choose a different target. Explain 6 of 8 without extrapolating beyond the sample or inventing interview cross-tabs.
2. **Promise.** Write a positioning statement, then draw up an evidence ledger for two claims. Identify one promise you should leave out or qualify.
3. **Offer.** State the fee, what it buys, and at least one non-price burden. Reconcile contribution, fixed cost and capacity. If the pilot needs a subsidy, say so rather than assuming visitors become payments.
4. **Channel.** Design a small comparison with permission, accessibility and equal availability. State the primary measure, anticipated interference and stop rule. Do not reuse real contact lists or collect personal information in the exercise.
5. **Measurement.** Draw a dashboard that separates contacts, visits, requests and fulfilled loans. Calculate the stage rates and repeat-use rates, show denominators, and name what neither rate can establish. Recommend a reversible change and one observation that would make you revise it.

Judge the work on segment and promise fit, evidence for costs and capacity, a defensible comparison and honest reporting of unknowns. Cosmetic polish is not evidence. Complete the first version unaided, then use the [worked review](pilot-key.md). If you ask AI to critique one decision, retain the original, list any unsupported additions, verify its calculations separately and explain what you changed. Do a final unaided transfer: if each window loses two pickup slots, can the $11 fee cover the stated costs even if every available slot is filled?
