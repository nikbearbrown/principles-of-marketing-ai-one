# Choose a channel you can test

Two announcements say the same thing in different places. One gets more page visits. The other gets more pickups. Which helped? Without a defined audience, a comparable offer and a usable path from message to pickup, the question may not have a clean answer.

You will choose a channel for a reachable audience, design a small comparison and preserve consent and accessibility while measuring response.

## Name what changes

The library wants to compare an opt-in email with an opt-in event reminder. Use the same promise, eligibility, hours and price. Use distinct comparable windows with the same kit capacity and staffing, or design an allocation that protects each comparison group from the other consuming its slots. In a true randomized comparison, people would need to be eligible for both contact methods, assignable without contact overlap, and able to receive the offer in comparable conditions. The fictional logs here are not such a trial. The people reached through each channel may differ at baseline.

Write the first touch in plain terms. Say what is available, where and when, and how to reserve without surrendering unrelated personal information. Use descriptive link text, captions or text equivalents where appropriate, and a usable alternative if a reader cannot access the web form. No outreach is part of the exercise. In a real campaign, check actual permission and organizational rules first.

| Check before sending | Failure the plan prevents |
| --- | --- |
| Confirm permission for the chosen contact method and offer a way to stop messages | Treating a contact list as blanket consent |
| Count people once at the start of each eligible cohort | Inflating reach through repeated messages |
| Keep sufficient kit and counter capacity available | Mistaking lack of stock for lack of interest |
| Check keyboard access and readable pickup instructions | Measuring whether a form excluded readers rather than whether the offer fits |

## Choose where to learn

A visit can diagnose whether a message sends people to the offer. A completed handoff tracks actual use. A complaint or cancelled reservation can reveal an obstacle that a favorable conversion number hides. Keep failure states visible. Set one primary outcome before seeing results: fulfilled loans per eligible contacts within the assigned window. Use intermediate rates to ask where to improve, not to search for a favorable number.

## Try it

Suppose the email says “reserve now” but the other channel says “learn more.” Can a difference in reservation rates identify the effect of the channel? What would you hold constant?

**Check.** The comparison mixes medium and call to action. Match the promise and requested action, then confirm the same paths and comparable opportunities before interpreting a difference. If that cannot be done, describe the comparison as a difference between bundled workflows.
