# Make a promise you can check

A library advertises “all the repair support you'll ever need.” Its offer is one kit, a staffed pickup window, and no technician. The problem is not a weak adjective. It is a claim the service cannot keep.

You will choose a target, compare the offer with the customer's next-best alternative and write a positioning statement that has evidence behind it.

## Choose whom to serve first

Targeting chooses which proposed segment should receive an offer now. Start with nearby renters who already plan one basic bicycle adjustment, can use the existing pickup window, and prefer not to store a full set of tools. The library has a portable kit and an existing counter. That is an operational fit, not proof this is the largest group or the most lucrative one.

A competing product is not the only alternative. A neighbor may lend the tools for free but without a reliable time. A shop may supply expertise at a different price. Postponement avoids payment today and leaves the problem unresolved. Ask what the person would actually do absent this offer. If postponement is common, outperforming a specialist retailer may be beside the point.

**Positioning** is the intended place of the offer relative to those alternatives in the reader's decision. It is not another word for brand color or a catchy headline. One candidate statement:

> For nearby renters planning a basic bicycle adjustment who do not want to own another set of tools, the branch offers a reservable repair kit for pickup during its staffed window. It is an alternative to buying a full set or waiting for a neighbor's tools.

The claim still needs verification. Check the kit contents, booking record, pickup hours and borrower terms before printing it. Do not promise repairs beyond what the kit actually supports. A clear “tools only, no repair service” can improve fit even if it reduces clicks.

## Trace each claim to evidence

| Draft claim | Evidence to check | What not to say yet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reservable kit | Booking flow permits advance reservation and shows available slots | “Always available.” |
| Carry-home kit | Measured weight, dimensions and a practical carrying check | “Anyone can carry it easily.” |
| Lower ownership burden | No storage or ownership requirement after returning it | “Cheaper than every alternative.” |

Treat this as an evidence ledger. A writer can improve the wording, but cannot invent an availability check. A marketing team should delete or mark an unsupported claim before making it more persuasive.

## Try it

Write one positioning statement for a different group, then name the capacity it would require. If the target needs hands-on diagnosis, a new message alone is insufficient. A qualified help session would be a different service with a different constraint. Do not claim it as a feature of the current kit.
