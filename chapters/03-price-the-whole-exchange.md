# Price the whole exchange

The proposed borrowing fee is $9. An analyst projects 15 loans and says the pilot breaks even. The counter can hand out only eight kits in the window. Before debating whether the fee is attractive, ask how many exchanges can actually occur.

You will calculate a contribution and a capacity-constrained gap, and explain why the customer's cost includes effort as well as the fee.

## Separate the unit from the period

For this fictional pilot assume each fulfilled loan incurs $4 of wear and consumables and $3 of handling. There is $30 of extra fixed session cost. Existing donated equipment and long-run replacement costs are outside this incremental illustration. No inventory sale, late fees, taxes or unpaid balances are modeled. At a $9 collected fee, contribution per fulfilled loan is $9 − $7 = $2. The pilot needs $30 / $2 = 15 fulfilled loans to cover these stated costs.

At the eight-loan ceiling, receipts are $72, variable cost is $56, and fixed cost is $30. The pilot falls $14 short even if every slot is used. Counting reserves or website visits as paid fulfillments does not fix it. One constrained pilot can be worthwhile with a deliberate subsidy, but label who bears the subsidy and what the team will learn.

At a $11 fee contribution becomes $4; eight loans would yield $32 contribution, or $2 above fixed costs. That is conditional arithmetic. It does not establish eight people will borrow at $11. Demand and affordability are separate empirical questions.

## Distribution is part of the offer

A renter may spend twenty minutes walking to a branch and another trip returning the kit. A $9 offer with an inaccessible pickup can cost more in effort than borrowing from a neighbor. A staffed window simplifies handover checks and restricts availability. An alternate pickup point may reduce travel but add monitoring or reconciliation costs. Product, price, place and message constrain one another. Treat them as a design, not four independent boxes.

Do not hide a refundable deposit in the fee. A required deposit can tie up cash for the borrower even if it is later returned; it is not contribution in this model. State the rental terms and return process at the point of decision.

## Try it

At a $10 fee and six fulfilled loans, how far is the pilot from covering its explicit fixed costs? Would a wider message campaign alone close the gap if capacity stays eight?

**Check.** Contribution is $3 per loan; six loans contribute $18, leaving $12 uncovered. At eight, contribution is $24, leaving $6 uncovered. No amount of broader reach changes that ceiling. Revisit the cost, fee, subsidy or feasible fulfillment capacity before claiming viability.
